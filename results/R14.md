# R14: what the S6 corpus costs, split into its parts

**Date:** 2026-09-26. **Plan:** `.claude/plans/s5-first-s6-cost-split.md`.
**Branch:** `description-reading`.
**Spend:** $0.80: one secure-cloud A40 at $0.49/hr for 1 h 40 min, three
training runs plus two extra evaluations. The balance went from $14.17 to
$13.38.
**Everything the pod wrote is on the laptop** in
`models/tiny/runs/pod_s6split/`: each run's `best.pt`, `last.pt`, config and
curve, every generation, and both console logs. All 43 files were checked
against the pod's listing by size before the pod was deleted.

## The result

1. **S6's three generator options are what cost the points.** Those options
   are decoys on every row, uniform twin roles, and opaque names on 15% of
   rows. The other suspects are cleared:
   - **Cache settings:** they help rather than hurt.
   - **Theme rewrite:** training on it scores best of all.

   A planner trained on S6's own themes with the three options **off**
   reaches **79.5%** on the S6 plain exam, against **47.1%** with them on. That
   comparison holds everything else fixed: same themes, cache settings,
   architecture and recipe. The gap is 32 points, larger than R13's 23.
2. **The options bought the plain planner almost nothing on the exams they
   were built for.** On S6's decoyed exam, A0 trained with them scores 38.6%
   against 37.4% without them. On the flip probe it is 26.0% against 22.6%.
3. **The options caused most of R12/R13's dominant failure.** Calling the
   wrong entity's tool is the first mistake in **47.2%** of tasks for A0
   trained on S6. It falls to **9.8%** without the options, close to its level
   before S6 (13–19%). Handoff item 3 is therefore mostly answered: a residual
   of about 10% remains.
4. **The reading stages read, and it only shows when decoys are present.**
   Trained on S5's rows, SPt does no better than A0 on the plain exam (71.7%
   against 73.9%, inside the noise). On S6's audited decoyed exam it scores
   **44.1% against 32.8%**, and on the flip probe 18.3% against 13.0%. Neither
   model saw a decoy in training.
5. **A new best planner for unseen worlds:** A0 on S6 with the options off,
   `models/tiny/runs/pod_s6split/runs/s6off_A0/best.pt` on
   `models/tiny/data_cache_s6off`. It scores 79.5% on the S6 plain exam. The
   previous best was 70.4% (R13's S5 diagnostic).

## Terms

- **Rows:** the generated training tasks.
  - **S5**: `data/s5_plain.jsonl`, the old themes.
  - **S6**: `data/s6_train.jsonl`, the rewritten themes with the three
    options on.
  - **S6 options-off**: `data/s6off_train.jsonl`, new for this run: the
    rewritten themes with all three options off, generated by
    `results/logs/gen_s6off.sh`. It has 30,000 rows, 21.9 tools per row, and
    no decoyed or opaque-name rows (checked from the rows' tags).
- **Cache settings:** what `prep.py` does when it turns rows into tensors:
  - the tokenizer
  - whether tool names are on the line
  - how many characters of each description are kept
  - the line length

  "S6 settings" means `data_cache_s6g`'s: names on, 120 characters, R12's
  general-English tokenizer, 112-token lines.
- **A0:** the plain planner. **SPt:** the planner with R12's V2 reading stages
  loaded and fine-tuned. Both use R10's recipe: `ar`, seed 0, 12 epochs,
  batch 64, pad weight 0.5, packed.
- **Exams:** all use the 42 worlds no training set contains, and every number
  is goal success.
  - **S6 plain:** 2,000 undecoyed tasks.
  - **S6 decoyed:** the same kind of tasks with authored decoys. This is the
    exam that passes the style audit (R11).
  - **Flip probe:** every answer is a sibling the theme did not author as the
    tool.
  - **S5 plain / S5 decoyed:** R10's exams. The decoyed one uses template
    decoys that a style reader can pass (R10 §8).

## Why the question was open

R13's comparison changed four cache settings as well as the rows. The S5
model's cache had no names, 60-character descriptions, an S5-theme tokenizer
and 80-token lines; the S6 model's had S6 settings. The no-names run fixed
only one of the four. **Run 1** holds the settings fixed and changes only the
rows. **Run 3** holds the themes fixed and changes only the options.

## The runs

| run | model | rows | cache |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A0 | S5 | `data_cache_s5g` (S6 settings) |
| 2 | SPt | S5 | `data_cache_s5g` |
| 3 | A0 | S6 options-off | `data_cache_s6off` (S6 settings) |

Both new caches use `data_cache_s6g`'s tokenizer file, and it is
byte-identical (md5). Their configs differ from `data_cache_s6g`'s only in the
corpus, exam, layout and `max_slots` fields.

`data_cache_s5g`'s exam is `data/s5s6_holdout.jsonl`: S5 plain, S5 decoyed,
and S6 plain with ids suffixed `+s6`. Those `+s6` rows are the same 4,000 tasks
as `data/s6_holdout.jsonl`, in the same order (checked).

To put runs 1–2 on S6's decoyed and flip exams without retraining, an
evaluation-only cache was built: `data_cache_s5g_s6dec`. It has S5's rows,
`data_cache_s5g`'s exact layout (178 joint ids, the same `keywords.json`), and
S6's decoyed and flip rows. The 19 rows (of 4,400) that carry more than 54
tools, the most that layout holds, were left out.

Every comparison below is restricted to the task ids that all the compared
files share (`results/logs/s6split_matched.py`,
`results/logs/s6split/matched.json`).

## S6 plain exam (2,000 tasks)

Scored in today's tree.

- **Whole tasks:** 1,654 tasks.
- **Paused segments:** 346 pieces of tasks that stop to wait for data.
- **Wrong entity:** the share of all tasks whose first mistake is calling
  another entity's tool (`fail_kinds.py`).

| model | rows | cache | goal | whole tasks | paused segments | wrong entity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A0 (R13 diagnostic) | S5 | old settings | 70.4% | 72.7% | 59.5% | 19.2% |
| **run 1: A0** | S5 | S6 settings | **73.9%** | 77.4% | 56.9% | 13.3% |
| **run 2: SPt** | S5 | S6 settings | 71.7% | 72.4% | 67.9% | 13.9% |
| **run 3: A0** | **S6 options-off** | S6 settings | **79.5%** | 79.3% | **80.1%** | **9.8%** |
| A0 (R13) | S6 | S6 settings | 47.1% | 45.5% | 54.6% | 47.2% |
| A0, no names (R13) | S6 | S6 settings, no names | 49.2% | 49.0% | 50.3% | n/a |
| SPt (R13) | S6 | S6 settings | 58.6% | 58.3% | 60.1% | 36.0% |

- **Cache settings: +3.5.** Run 1 against the R13 diagnostic: same rows, only
  the cache settings differ. They are not the cost.
- **Rows at fixed settings: −26.8.** Run 1 against A0 on S6. This is R13's
  finding, now with the settings held fixed.
- **Themes: +5.6, and it is paused tasks.** Run 3 against run 1. On whole
  tasks the two runs differ by 1.9 points, within noise. On paused segments
  run 3 is 23 points ahead. The reason is that today's sandbox cannot run
  S5's paused tasks, whose tools were renamed in `a03610e`. So `prep.py` drops
  them, and S5 trains on 110 paused segments against the options-off rows'
  5,022. R13's S5 run had the same gap.
- **The three options: −32.4.** Run 3 against A0 on S6. Same themes, cache
  settings and recipe; only the options differ. See caveat 2.

## S6 decoyed exam and flip probe

These are the exams the options were built to improve.

| model | rows | S6 decoyed (1,987 ids) | flip probe (469 ids) |
|---|---|---|---|
| run 1: A0 | S5 | 32.8% | 13.0% |
| run 2: SPt | S5 | **44.1%** | 18.3% |
| run 3: A0 | S6 options-off | 37.4% | 22.6% |
| A0 (R13) | S6 | 38.6% | 26.0% |
| A0, no names (R13) | S6 | 39.5% | 26.4% |
| SPt (R13) | S6 | **50.4%** | **38.8%** |

- **For A0, the options buy about 1 point on the decoyed exam** (37.4% →
  38.6%) and 3.4 on the flip probe, while costing 32 on the plain exam.
- **For SPt, decoy training is worth more.** SPt on S6 reaches 50.4%,
  against SPt on S5's 44.1%. That is suggestive but not clean, because the
  rows' themes differ as well. The clean version is the first "next run"
  below.
- **Decoy choices:** in `evaluate.py`'s count of decoyed tasks where the
  program called a decoy, run 1 A0 called one in 65.5% of them and run 2 SPt
  in 46.5%.

## S5 exams (runs 1–2)

Scored from the `../covenant-pre-a03610e` worktree, as R13 was. Its decoyed
rows carry their own sandbox and scored in today's tree.

| | A0, old settings (R13) | run 1: A0 | run 2: SPt |
|---|---|---|---|
| S5 plain | 76.9% | 82.5% | **83.6%** |
| S5 decoyed | 36.8% | 43.3% | **65.7%** |
| S5 test (training worlds) | 97.7% | 97.9% | 97.4% |

SPt's +22 on S5's decoyed exam is larger than its +11 on S6's. A style reader
can pass S5's decoyed exam, so read the S6 number as the reading effect and
the S5 number as an upper bound. Run 3's test split, on training worlds, is
96.3%. R13's S6-trained A0 scored 85.8% there, so the options make even the
training worlds harder.

## Caveats

1. **One seed per run.** R9 measured the planner's seed spread at 1.8
   points. Findings 1–3 are 20–37 point effects. The SPt-against-A0 plain
   difference in run 1 against run 2 (−2.2) is not a finding.
2. **Corrected 2026-09-26 (`.claude/plans/decoy-fraction.md`): this caveat
   was wrong.** `results/logs/gen_s6today.sh` regenerated S6's training rows
   with today's generator, and all 28,739 match the original shards exactly
   (same MD5). The original text follows. **Today's generator does not
   reproduce the S6 shards.** They were written
   about an hour before the commit that recorded the generator (`ec9c20f`);
   only a comment has changed in `data/gen/` since then. So run 3 against A0
   on S6 also differs by whatever changed in that window. The rows' structure
   matches S6 (same 104 worlds, same level mix), and a 32-point effect from an
   undocumented change in that hour is not plausible. Still, it is not ruled
   out.
3. **Which of the three options costs the points is not split here.** Decoys
   on every row remain the lead suspect, since the plain exam has none. Every
   S6 training row carries 45–52 tools, against the exam's 22.
4. **SPt's best-of-4 generation did not run on the pod.** Its compile check
   for paused segments needs the sandbox, which the bundle leaves out
   (traceback in `results/logs/s6split/s5g_SPt.log`). The greedy
   numbers above are the comparison. R13 got about +2 from best-of-4.

## Next runs (none started)

1. **SPt on the options-off rows** (`data_cache_s6off`), about $0.30. This is
   the obvious candidate for the best model: run 3's plain score plus the
   stages' decoy reading.
2. **Put the options back one at a time on the options-off rows,** for A0
   and SPt:
   - decoys on a fraction of rows (say 25%)
   - decoys on every row
   - twin roles
   - opaque names

   This splits finding 1 and shows how much decoy training SPt can take
   before the plain score drops. It needs a generation and a cache build per
   arm, about an hour of laptop time each, with pod runs of about $0.27 per
   arm.
3. **The residual wrong-entity failure (about 10% of tasks)** is now small
   enough to study in the failed tasks themselves rather than by retraining.
