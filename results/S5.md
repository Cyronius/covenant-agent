## 1. What worked

**The decoy gap is gone: 9.25 points → 0.** Same 400 tasks with and without
two to four description-only sibling tools per mutating tool; 99.5% either
way. On S4 the decoyed half lost 39 tasks and **37 of them compiled and ran**
— the model called a do-nothing sibling and reported success. Family B's
6,000 rows bought exactly the property they were written for, and it is the
cleanest result of the retrain.

**The abstain control went 83.75 → 99.2.** `R6.md` §0.2 traced its −16.25 to
one distribution: the corpus paired `ABORT NOT_FOUND` with a referent 2,597
times against 195 bare aborts, so on L11's tasks — whose record name reaches
no constant, leaving nothing to filter on and nothing to name — the model
reached for `AMBIGUOUS F4` or the type-invalid `NOT_FOUND F6`. S5 leaves the
name unextracted 40% of the time (1,168 bare against 1,496 with a referent)
and the miss closes.

**E-known clears its bar at 97.2.** The 42 misses S4 had were one idiom: a
field read off a `MOST` result, which is the winner's id. S5 teaches the
re-fetch in 798 rows against S4's 8, and on the argmax question exam the
model re-fetches on 9 of 9.

**`IN` is adopted.** 30 of 30 tasks whose reference needs it are written with
it, on 30 reserved domains, and the whole 60-task new-forms exam scores
100%. Spec 0.6.0 earns its place: the form was unsayable a day ago, 2,664
corpus rows teach it, and the model reaches for it on worlds it never saw.
Adoption was already 29/30 at checkpoint 500 — a tenth of one epoch.

**The coursebuilder app: whole-turn 12% → 49%.** Family C's held-out world.
Every turn compiles and the model's whole turn matches the oracle's on 59 of
120, against S4's 14.

## 2. What did not

**Family E did not transfer, and the way it failed is the point.**

| e_recovery, 100 tasks each | goal |
|---|---|
| `NOT_FOUND` | 98 |
| `PERMISSION_DENIED` | **0** |
| `RATE_LIMITED` | **0** |

The prompt for these tasks *contains the failure*: the program that ran, the
rendered `RUNTIME PERMISSION_DENIED ... injected on cap_plc`, the calls that
ran and their ok/error flags. The corpus contains 1,000 rows of each shape
with the right targets (`ABORT UNSUPPORTED`; `TRY RETRY 3`). And on all 200
tasks **the model's first line is a plain `CALL` that re-issues the call
that just failed.** Not one `TRY`, not one `ABORT`.

`NOT_FOUND` passes because its correct continuation — list, filter by name,
act — *is* the model's dominant template. So this is not "recovery is 33%
learned". It is: the model does not read the failure block at all, and
scores whenever the right answer happens to coincide with what it would
have written anyway. That is `R4.md`'s inert react rule, now measured after
a corpus deliberately built to teach the opposite.

**The generalization suites sag 1–2.5 points.** E-foreign −1.0, E-crowded
−1.3, crowded-v2 −1.0, OOD-English −2.5 (which drops it below its ≥90 bar),
real `route_write` −3.75. Nothing collapsed, but the trade is visible: a
corpus a third of which is new families and 23% of which is episodes buys
the targeted fixes and costs a little of the breadth S4 had. Whether that is
the family rows crowding out theme variety or the two recipe changes
shifting L4/L11 is not separable from this run alone.

**The dungeon still wins nothing.** 0/3, and the funnel's first gate is shut
for the fourth checkpoint running.

## 3. The per-turn picture, and why the dungeon number is not progress

3 episodes, 120 turns a world, each checkpoint on its own surface
(`results/logs/perturn.sh`; S4's row is `FAMILIES.md` §3):

| | S4 first tool / whole turn | S5 first tool / whole turn |
|---|---|---|
| dungeon | 1% / 0% | **98%** / 0% |
| house | 49% / 0% | 28% / **7%** |
| coursebuilder app | 93% / 12% | 49% / **49%** |

The dungeon's 1% → 98% looks like the headline and is not. S4 could not
compile there (109 of 120 turns died in the typechecker); S5 compiles 117 of
120 and names the oracle's tool almost every turn — and then **111 of its
135 calls are invalid**. It has learned which verb the situation calls for
and not which argument. Whole-turn agreement is still 0, and it still never
reaches the key.

The house is the opposite shape: first-tool agreement *fell* to 28%, yet
whole-turn agreement rose from 0 to 7% and **the way opened in 3 of 3
episodes** — the first funnel movement any checkpoint has produced on that
world. Picking the oracle's opening tool and picking a turn that works are
different things, which is why `FAMILIES.md` §3 says to read the last
column.

The app's first-tool, first-tool+arg and whole-turn numbers are all 59/120:
when it acts it acts exactly right, and the other 61 turns it does nothing
useful. Its funnel did not move (`any_value_set` 0/3), so 49% whole-turn
agreement is not yet a finished form.

## 4. What to do next

1. **The react failure is the biggest open item**, and it is now measured
   rather than inferred. 2,000 rows taught the two error-code continuations
   and bought nothing; more rows of the same shape are unlikely to help. The
   question is whether the model can attend to the failure block at all —
   a probe on a capable model (the 27B, `R4.md`'s template) would say
   whether this is a size problem or a corpus problem before another
   retrain pays for the answer.
2. **OOD-English and `route_write` are below their bars.** Both are breadth
   metrics and both moved down together; the cheap check is whether the
   family rows displaced theme variety, which a mix with the families capped
   lower would answer.
3. **The classic control** (`R6.md` §0.3) is now worth running: E-known
   cleared 97, so the question the control settles — whether the typed
   letters cost anything — is no longer confounded by the recipe gaps.
4. The field-vs-field clause from spec 0.5.0 still has no corpus rows and
   cannot get any until theme entities carry a second comparable field
   (`FAMILIES.md` §6).
