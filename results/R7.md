# R7 — structural binding: the 9.4M tiny planner binds symbols, and writes working programs

**Status:** step 1 of `.claude/plans/npu-native-planner.md` passes its gate, by a
margin that makes the gate uninteresting. Tool-slot accuracy went from 6% against
5.6% chance (R3) to 99.6% ± 0.1 on the test split across three seeds. Compile rate
went from 8.6% to 99.9%. Everything downstream of it now depends on numbers closer
to a ceiling than to a floor.

**One number does not survive the seed spread, and it is the one that matters.**
On the held-out world the control arm scores 96.4%, 92.0% and 66.2% goal success
across seeds 0, 1 and 2 — 84.9% ± 16.3. The third seed is indistinguishable from
the others on the test split (98.8% goal, 99.6% tool slots) and loses its binding
on the unseen world (83.6% tool slots, 73.1% compile). So binding transfers to
unseen declarations *usually*, and how well is a seed-level coin flip at this
size. The masked-diffusion arm is far tighter on the same axis: 80.0% ± 3.5.

**Date:** 2026-09-18, three seeds. **Plan:** `.claude/plans/npu-native-planner.md`, sequence
step 1; `.claude/plans/canvas-field-access-split.md` for the tokenizer half.
**Code:** `models/tiny/`, `--binding structural`. **Model:** 9.4M parameters,
width 256: a 2-layer per-line encoder, a 1-layer sparse schema-graph pass, a
2-layer turn encoder, a 4-layer decoder over a 64-slot canvas. Two arms differ in
one line, whether decoder self-attention is causal. **Data:** `data/s5_plain.jsonl`,
same 90/5/5 split as R3 and the same held-out world (`bookstore`). **Trained:**
12 epochs, batch 64, `pad_weight` 0.5, seeds 0, 1 and 2, on a rented GPU
(`models/tiny/run_step1.sh`). **Scored:** every program compiled by
`core.pipeline.build` and executed by `harness.run.run_task` against world state.
No number here comes from any other path.

## 1. What changed from R3

Three things, all representational, all in the plan's decisions 4, 6, 7 and 11.
Nothing about capacity: the model grew by 1.5M parameters, all of it in two extra
encoder layers.

- **One vector per schema line, not one token stream.** Each tool, field and
  constant line is encoded on its own; a sparse graph pass lets a tool see the
  fields its signature names and a field see its entity and its tools. The
  per-turn path reads about 50 line vectors plus the request instead of 1,100
  tokens of serialised schema.
- **Pointers, not a symbol vocabulary.** A canvas slot holds a keyword (62 fixed
  rows) or points at one of this task's line vectors, or at a register. There is
  no `T23` row to learn. An undeclared symbol has no vector and cannot be
  produced, which is the symbol half of the grammar for free.
- **`r0.F6` is two slots.** R3's output vocabulary held 115 register-dot-field
  compounds covering 708 of 2,064 field references, so a third of the field
  references were not symbol slots at all. Both bindings now split them
  (`corpus.program_tokens`), and the flat vocabulary fell from 500 entries to 401.

## 2. The numbers

Test split, 1,415 tasks. R3's run 1 is the same data, same scorer, 7.9M
parameters, flat binding.

Three seeds, mean and spread.

| | decoder passes | compile | goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| R3 control (one seed) | 29.1 | 8.6% | 8.3% |
| R3 diffusion, 32 steps (one seed) | 32.0 | 7.6% | 7.1% |
| **control** | 29.6 | **99.9% ± 0.1** | **98.2% ± 0.8** |
| **diffusion, 1 step** | 1.0 | 90.1% ± 0.6 | 86.2% ± 1.2 |
| diffusion, 8 steps | 8.0 | 90.8% ± 0.3 | 86.6% ± 0.9 |
| diffusion, 16 steps | 16.0 | 92.1% ± 0.5 | 87.1% ± 0.6 |
| diffusion, 32 steps | 32.0 | 94.7% ± 0.2 | 88.8% ± 0.4 |
| diffusion, 8 steps + repair | 8.6 | 94.4% ± 0.2 | 89.0% ± 1.0 |

One denoising pass reaches 88% of the control's goal success for a twenty-ninth of
the decoder work, and the compiler in the sampling loop is worth more than four
times the steps: 89.0% at 8.6 passes against 88.8% at 32.

Per-slot accuracy against the reference canvas, control, test split, seed 0, with
chance in the last column (the tool-slot spread across seeds is 0.1 point):

| slot kind | n | accuracy | chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| keyword | 25,272 | 98.9% | — |
| tool | 2,696 | **99.5%** | 5.6% |
| field | 2,064 | 94.1% | 4.8% |
| constant | 2,801 | 96.4% | 17.5% |
| register | 7,590 | 99.6% | — |

Line-aligned CALL agreement, control: 2,687 of 2,696 calls name the same tool as
the reference (99.7% against 5.6% chance) and 2,693 the same effect class (99.9%
against 27.2%). R3's equivalent sat at chance on both.

**The held-out world, which is the number that matters, and the one the seeds
disagree about.** 275 tasks in a world whose tools, fields and entities appear in
no training example:

| | seed 0 | seed 1 | seed 2 | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control, compile | 98.9% | 100.0% | 73.1% | 90.7% ± 15.2 |
| control, goal | 96.4% | 92.0% | 66.2% | **84.9% ± 16.3** |
| control, tool slots | 98.0% | 97.5% | 83.6% | 93.0% ± 8.2 |
| control, field slots | 93.6% | 91.4% | 81.2% | 88.7% ± 6.6 |
| diffusion k=8, compile | 87.6% | 87.6% | 81.5% | 85.6% ± 3.5 |
| diffusion k=8, goal | 82.5% | 81.5% | 76.0% | **80.0% ± 3.5** |
| diffusion k=8, tool slots | 97.7% | 97.7% | 93.9% | 96.4% ± 2.2 |

Two readings, and the second is the one to carry forward.

Binding does transfer. At the median seed the control gives up 6 points of goal
success going from worlds it trained on to a world it has never seen, and every
seed's tool-slot accuracy on the unseen world is 15 to 17 times chance.

But the transfer is unstable at this size, and only on the unseen world. Seed 2 is
the best of the three on the test split (98.8% goal) and the worst by 26 points on
the holdout, with its tool slots falling from 99.6% to 83.6% — the binding itself
partially failing to transfer, which is a different failure from getting a program
wrong. Nothing in training or in the test split shows it coming. The diffusion arm
does not have it to the same degree: ± 3.5 on holdout goal against the control's
± 16.3, which is the one axis where the parallel decode is ahead.

## 3. The control wins on the test split by 9 points, and much less than that elsewhere

R3 could not compare the arms, because both were at the floor. At 99% binding the
comparison is finally about generation order, and the left-to-right arm wins the
test split: 98.2% against 88.8% goal. More denoising steps close part of it (86.2%
at one step to 88.8% at 32) and the compiler in the sampling loop buys 2.4 points
at 8 steps, but nothing reaches the control.

On the unseen world the same comparison is 84.9% ± 16.3 against 80.0% ± 3.5 —
under 5 points apart, with the control's advantage inside its own seed spread and
the diffusion arm the steadier of the two. So "the control is better" is a claim
about the test split. Which arm generalises better is not settled by three seeds,
and the design's reason for choosing the parallel decode is passes per program,
which this does not touch.

The gap is concentrated, not spread. Levels 0 to 6, which are R3's pass bar:

| level | what it needs | control | diffusion, 32 steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | one call | 100% | 99% |
| 1 | a lookup then a call | 100% | 100% |
| 2 | filter then foreach | 98% | 78% |
| 3 | a compound filter predicate | **78%** | **16%** |
| 4 | field access into a call | 99% | 90% |
| 5 | an IF/ELSE branch | 96% | 67% |
| 6 | a format then a call | 100% | 79% |
| all 0 to 6 | | 96.2% | 76.7% |

Level 3 is a multi-term predicate: `FILTER r0 F12 LT D0 AND NOT F9 EQ B0 -> r1`.
Of the 19.5 points the diffusion arm gives up on levels 0 to 6, level 3 is 41 of
the 101 lost tasks, level 5 is 25, level 6 is 17 and level 2 is 11; levels 0, 1
and 4 cost it 8 between them.

### What the predicate failure actually is, measured rather than inferred

`diagnose.py --lines` attributes each program's first difference to a line, says
whether the line's *shape* or only its symbols is wrong, and says whether the
program reached the goal anyway. Four findings, in the order they change the
conclusion.

**One line carries the whole deficit.** Across the 1,415 test programs, FILTER is
the first differing line in 165 of the diffusion arm's 234 non-exact programs.
Nothing else reaches 25.

**A differing predicate means something different in each arm.** At level 3 the
control differs from the reference on FILTER in 50 of 67 tasks and still reaches
the goal in 36 of them: a reordered or differently-expressed predicate that means
the same thing. The diffusion arm differs in 61 of 67 and reaches the goal in 5.

**The diffusion arm's predicates break the grammar; the control's never do.**
`pred = clause {("AND"|"OR") clause}` with `clause = ["NOT"] field cmp (operand |
field)` (`spec/agent_core.md:95-99`) forbids two adjacent operators. The control
violates it zero times in 1,415 programs, because a left-to-right decoder cannot.
The diffusion arm violates it in about a third of level-3 programs: `AND AND`, a
comparator with a join on its left, `NOT EQ`. That check finds zero faults in the
813 reference predicates, so it is the grammar and not a proxy for it.

**Enforcing the grammar fixes the symptom and buys nothing.** The adjacency rules
are now in `sample.local_mask`, and the sampler commits slots one at a time in
descending confidence, recomputing the mask after each — without that it commits
about 13 slots in one step from marginals that never saw each other, which is
where `AND AND` came from. On the same checkpoint and the same tasks:

| | level 3 parse | level 3 compile | level 3 goal | test goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k=8, mask per step | 4.5% | 4.5% | 1.5% | 86.1% |
| k=8, mask per commit | 47.8% | 16.4% | 1.5% | 86.2% |

Ten times the parse rate, no additional correct answers. So the grammar violation
was a symptom, not the disease: the arm does not know the predicate, and making it
well-formed converts a parse error into a wrong answer. The rules stay because
they are free, because R3's bar is stated under full constrained decoding, and
because decision 8 puts the grammar in the kernel anyway — but with a correction
the design needs: **the mask has to be applied per committed slot, not once per
denoising pass.** A per-pass mask is the version that does nothing.

**What does help is passes, and the compiler helps more per pass.** Level 3, goal
success: 0.0% at k=1, 1.5% at k=8, 3.0% at k=16, 16.4% at k=32 — and 11.9% at
k=8 with the compiler in the sampling loop, which is 8.5 passes against 32. The
diffusion arm is still 61 points behind the control at k=32, so this is a
joint-distribution failure across the predicate that more denoising steps
partially resolve. Step 2 asks whether loops buy the same thing without the
dispatches; the compiler-in-the-loop result says a verifier is the cheaper lever
either way.

**Neither arm passes R3's bar.** The bar is 100% on levels 0 to 6 under full
constrained decoding at 40M parameters or less; the control is at 96.2% under the
grammar's symbol half only, with level 3 at 78%. It is close enough that the bar
is now a target rather than a question.

## 4. What this settles for the NPU design, and what it does not

- **Bet 1, that a small resident block can do the task at all, is no longer
  open at 9.4M.** The plan's measured size curve had 7.9M at chance and nothing
  working under 350M. A 9.4M model now writes working programs, including for a
  world it has never seen, at two seeds of three. The session-encode split is the
  reason, as the plan argued: the per-turn path never reads schema text. What is
  open is the stability of that transfer, which is a new question this run raised
  rather than one it inherited.
- **The block fits.** 4 decoder layers at width 256 is 4.21M parameters in the
  loop body: 1.00 MB at ternary, 2.01 MB at 4-bit, 4.02 MB at int8. Every format
  fits the 4 MB memory tiles, and at ternary the design has three quarters of the
  budget it has not spent.
- **The decode order is now a real decision rather than a preference.** The
  design chose masked diffusion for the NPU because it fills 8 slots per pass
  instead of one (0.7 to 4.5 ms against 4 to 27 ms in the plan's cost model).
  It costs 9 points of goal success on the test split and under 5 on the unseen
  world, where it is also the steadier arm across seeds. Whether the loop buys
  those points back is exactly step 2's question, and level 3 is where to look
  first.
- **New, and not in the plan's list of bets: transfer stability.** One seed of
  three loses its binding on the unseen world while looking perfect on the test
  split. Nothing downstream can tell those two kinds of seed apart without a
  held-out world, so every later step keeps one. Three seeds is the minimum that
  shows this at all, and it is a floor rather than a comfortable sample.
- **Untouched:** the crowded suites, retrieval, and everything on the NPU. The
  step-1 gate was the only thing this run was for.

## 5. What changed in the plan

- `PLAN.md` §14 records the gate as passed.
- Step 2's sweep is not run on this model: at 98.2% goal there is no headroom to
  show a loop helping. It runs on a one-layer block, which is both the shape with
  room to move and the shape the NPU wants (`models/tiny/run_step2.sh`).
- Decision 3's format argument is amended by measurement rather than by this run:
  `models/npu/kernels/tern_mk/README.md` found ternary does not dominate 4-bit on
  this silicon, because 4-bit's unpack is folded into the MAC by the hardware and
  ternary's is software at 0.0156 cycles per weight — a 12.5% tax at a 64-slot
  canvas for twice the resident parameters. So step 3 sweeps four formats at
  matched resident bytes rather than assuming ternary
  (`models/tiny/run_step3.sh`), and the kernel side has said all it can: the tax
  is small enough that capacity decides.
- **The pretrained text encoder is back within reach, on the condition the plan
  set for it.** That condition was a model that binds on test and fails on the
  held-out world, which is exactly seed 2. It is not triggered outright, because
  two seeds of three transfer fine and the failure looks like an optimisation
  accident rather than a missing capability, but the next cheap thing to try is
  whatever stabilises transfer -- more worlds, a schema-line dropout, an
  averaged run -- and a frozen pretrained encoder is one of the candidates rather
  than a withdrawn idea.
