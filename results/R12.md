# R12 — the vocabulary push: general English takes the stage past the teacher

**Date:** 2026-09-25. **Plan:** `.claude/plans/vocab-push.md`. **Branch:**
`description-reading`. **Follows:** R11 §3b–§3c. **Spend:** $0.54 (one RTX PRO
4000 Blackwell pod, secure cloud, $0.57/hr, about an hour).

## The result

The 2.26M-parameter description stage, still with a 4,096-piece tokenizer,
now reads which tool a request means **better than the 33M teacher does**:
**92.3%** against 89.9% on the exam's readable flip-slot decisions (2,309 of
them, ±0.65 at this n). Before this push it was 89.1%.

The gain is where R11 §3c said it would be, on the words training never
contained:

| 6 epochs of S6 | all decisions | deciding word seen in S6 training (1,792) | deciding word never in S6 training (517) |
|---|---|---|---|
| U2, R11 §3b | 89.1% | 91.2% | 81.8% |
| **V2**: 4,096 general-English pieces + general text | **92.3%** | 92.9% | **90.3%** |
| V2-30k: the teacher's 30,522 pieces + general text | 94.1% | 94.8% | 91.5% |
| teacher, zero-shot | 89.9% | 89.0% | 92.8% |

Misses on unseen deciding words fell from 18.2% to 9.7%. At 4-bit the stage
reads 92.4% (int8 92.3%): rounding still costs nothing (`quant_v2_6ep.json`).
Over all look-alike decisions, not just the flip slots, it went from 80.7% to
87.0%.

Every number here is from each run's **last** checkpoint, never one picked on
the exam. One mild selection remains: V2 was chosen over V1 and V3 by their
3-epoch exam scores before its 6-epoch run.

## Terms, once

- **Deciding word**: a word in the right tool's description that none of its
  look-alikes' descriptions contain (R11 §3c). "Unseen" = at least one of them
  never appears in the S6 training corpus's requests, descriptions or names
  (9,407 distinct words). The split is fixed by S6, so it compares across
  tokenizers.
- **General text**: 587,888 sentences and 235,286 names from WordNet (glosses
  and examples), Google API Discovery, AWS botocore documentation and our
  imported open_pairs tool text (`data/gen/general_corpus.py`,
  `data/general/README.md`). Nothing from the 42 exam worlds; no corpus text
  shares an 8-word run with any exam request, description or theme string
  (checked: 0 of 587,888, with a positive control).

## What each part was worth (3 epochs, compared with U1 = the old recipe at 3 epochs)

| arm | tokenizer | general text | dictionary look-alikes | all | seen | unseen | dictionary test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U1 (R11) | 4,096, theme text | — | — | 88.2% | 90.5% | 80.3% | — |
| V1 | 4,096, general English | — | — | 87.1% | 88.2% | 83.2% | 26.5% |
| **V2** | 4,096, general English | yes | — | **90.9%** | **92.3%** | **86.3%** | 32.4% |
| V3 | 4,096, general English | yes | yes | 89.6% | 90.8% | 85.1% | 36.8% |
| V2-30k | teacher's 30,522 | yes | — | 92.1% | 93.6% | 87.0% | 37.1% |
| teacher | | | | 89.9% | 89.0% | 92.8% | 53.3% |

1. **The tokenizer alone is not the fix.** Learning the 4,096 pieces from
   general English (V1) cut pieces per word on general text from 2.12 to 1.64
   and helped unseen words (+2.9), but cost seen words (−2.3): theme words
   lost the whole-word pieces they had. Net −1.1.
2. **General text is.** Training the stages to reproduce the teacher's
   vectors for general text, then mixing it into S6 training (V2), is +3.8
   over V1 and +2.7 over U1, and helps seen and unseen words alike. With a
   small vocabulary the layers have to learn what the pieces add up to, and
   this is where they learn it.
3. **Dictionary look-alikes did not help the exam** (V3 −1.3 against V2),
   although they raise the dictionary test itself (+4.4). Not adopted. The
   likely cause is competition for the stage's capacity or the loss weight
   (a quarter-batch every general step); not investigated.
4. **The small vocabulary costs about 1.2–1.8 points** (V2-30k over V2: +1.2
   at 3 epochs, +1.8 at 6). A 30k table would be 3.9M parameters against the
   2.26M stage, kept on the host. Whether the points are worth that is a
   design call; nothing here forces it.
5. **More epochs still pay.** V2 went from 90.9% (3 epochs) to 92.3% (6), with
   its mid-run curve flat over the last 1,700 of 8,064 steps (93.3 → 93.1% on
   the 2,000-row subset), so 6 epochs is near this recipe's plateau.

## What is still short

- **Word meaning in isolation.** On held-out WordNet families (pick a word's
  definition among its siblings' definitions; chance 20.7%, 7,077 items) the
  stage reads 32%, the teacher 53%. The stage learned general English well
  enough to read tool descriptions, not the dictionary itself.
- **Unseen words, 2.5 points behind the teacher** (90.3% vs 92.8%). On seen
  words the stage is 3.9 points ahead.
- **The exam's defects** (R11 §3c: ambiguous requests, `readable` admitting a
  bare "send", one wrong label) are unchanged and cap everyone somewhere
  around 98–99%.

## Vocabulary size and width (2026-09-25, later; $0.42)

Four more 6-epoch arms of the V2 recipe side by side on one pod
(`models/tiny/run_size.sh`), one of them V2 itself with a different seed, so
the spread between two identical runs is on the table:

| arm | pieces | width (both stages) | parameters | all | seen | unseen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V2, seed 0 (above) | 4,096 | 128 | 2.26M | 92.3% | 92.9% | 90.3% |
| V2, seed 1 | 4,096 | 128 | 2.26M | 93.5% | 94.6% | 89.6% |
| v8k | 8,192 | 128 | 3.30M | 93.5% | 94.4% | 90.5% |
| w256 | 4,096 | 256 | 6.87M | 93.5% | 94.6% | 89.7% |
| v8k_w256 | 8,192 | 256 | 8.97M | 94.0% | 95.1% | 89.9% |

**Two runs of the same recipe differ by 1.2 points.** Every size change lands
within 0.5 of the seed-1 run, well inside that spread. On this exam, neither
doubling the vocabulary nor doubling the width (nor both, at four times the
parameters) shows a gain that can be told apart from a reseed. The 8k
tokenizer does fit general English better (1.44 pieces per word vs 1.64) and
reads unseen words 0.9 higher, but at n=517 that is well under one standard
error (±1.3).

Kept: **4,096 pieces, 128 wide** (the smallest), with the recipe's own result
best stated as 92.3–93.5% over two seeds against the teacher's 89.9%. Telling
the sizes apart would take several seeds per arm (at ~$0.10 an arm-run on one
pod, three seeds of all four is about $1.20). Logs and reports:
`results/logs/size/`; weights: `models/tiny/runs/pod_size/runs/size/`.

A note for every comparison in R11 and R12: a 1.2-point seed spread means
single-run differences of a point or so (U1 vs U2, V2 vs V3, the 30k
vocabulary's +1.8) are suggestive, not settled. Only the general-text effect
(+2.7 over U1 at 3 epochs, +3.2 to +4.4 at 6) clearly exceeds it.

## What the planner gets wrong (R9, before the schema-graph fix)

To decide whether another planner run is worth it, the last planner whose
generations survive (R9: 3 seeds × 2,000 exam tasks per half,
`models/tiny/out/ho42_ar_s*`) was broken down by the first place each failed
program departs from its reference (`models/tiny/fail_kinds.py`;
`results/logs/fail_kinds_r9_{plain,decoy}.json`). A program that does not
compile is classified by that same first departure: the compile error is
almost always downstream of it.

| first departure | plain: share of failures (29.6% of tasks failed) | decoyed: share of failures (54.0% failed) |
|---|---|---|
| **wrong tool, another signature** (another entity's tool in the same request) | **53.3%** | 40.2% |
| wrong tool, a look-alike (same signature: twin or decoy) | 0.5% | **33.7%** |
| wrong field | 24.2% | 11.0% |
| wrong keyword (mostly the `EFFECTS` header, WRITE ↔ DELETE) | 10.6% | 6.3% |
| everything else (comparisons, lengths, registers, constants) | ~11% | ~9% |

- **The largest class is not the look-alike problem R11/R12 worked on.** It
  is choosing which entity to start from when the request names two:
  "email every shipper that has a non-hazmat shipment" should list
  *shipments*, filter on hazmat and go to their shippers; the planner lists
  *shippers*. Choosing needs to know which entity carries the filtered field
  (hazmat is a shipment field), which is exactly the tool → field link R11
  §2a found missing from R9's and R10's schema graph. No planner has been
  trained since that fix.
- **Look-alikes are a third of the decoyed failures**: the part the reading
  stages address.
- **75% of failed plain programs (1,328 of 1,775) do not compile**, nearly all
  UNKNOWN_FIELD / TYPE_ERROR after a wrong starting entity. The typechecker
  catches them; nothing uses that yet (e.g. sample several programs, keep the
  first that compiles).
- **The keyword class mostly disappears on its own**: the `EFFECTS` header
  was dropped from the IR (agent-loop plan step 1b).
- **By level** (plain): 17 and 18 ~100%; 0, 13, 15, 16 over 90%; worst are the
  multi-step, multi-entity levels: 3 (38.9%), 7 (46.4%), 9 (47.2%), 2 and 6
  (50%).

This corrects a claim made while planning this step: the plain-task failures
are not "unrelated to reading"; most begin with the wrong tool, just not a
look-alike.
