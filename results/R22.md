# R22: a 9.5M ternary reader in front of A0 gives the first tiny planner that is best on every exam

**Date:** 2026-09-29. **Branch:** `description-reading`. **Spend:** $0.99
(one A40, four runs at once, about 1h50m); balance $10.05.
**Plan:** `.claude/plans/npu-planner.md`, phase 2. The owner: "No cerebras
but yes let's continue otherwise", so no 27B labels were used; everything is
learned from the training rows.

## The result

1. **The reader works, with no decoy training at all.** A0 with the frozen
   Ternlight-mini reader, on the options-off rows (`s6off_RD`), against R14's
   A0 on the same rows:
   - decoys the request decides: right 85% of the time, up from 45%;
   - decoyed exam: 48.8%, up from 37.4%;
   - flip probe: 43.4%, up from 22.7%;
   - plain exam: 76.7%, level with A0's two seeds (79.5% / 77.2%, spread
     2.3).
2. **With the decidable decoy rows, one model is now best or level on every
   exam.** That is A0 + reader on 25% flip-slot decoy rows the request
   decides (`fdc25_RD`):

   | exam | `fdc25_RD` | best before this | 27B, direct | 27B, reasoning first |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | plain | **79.5%** | 79.5% (A0 options-off) | 50.7% | 74.0% |
   | plain + backoff | **84.0%** | 84.5% (A0 options-off) | — | — |
   | decoyed | **51.4%** | 50.9% (SPt, 50% S6) | 42.3% | 62.0% |
   | flip probe | **45.8%** | 40.5% (SPt, 50% S6) | 46.0% | — |

   Until now no model was best on both the plain and the decoyed exams
   (R17). This one gives up nothing on plain.
3. **Its tool choice is the best of any tiny planner:**
   - lookalike choices 91.4% right (A0 88.4%, the 27B 97%);
   - decidable decoys 91.0% (A0 45%, the 27B 98%).
4. **The reader credits the right thing.** The same rows without the reader
   (`fdc25_A0`) are only a little better than A0 on decoys: decidable decoys
   55%, decoyed exam 40.7%, flip 28.6%. Adding the reader moves those to 91%,
   51.4% and 45.8%. The rows alone don't teach decoy reading to A0; the
   reader does.
5. **What's still missing is the "which tool is the normal one" sense,** the
   decoys no request decides.
   - Every model trained without list/fetch decoys guesses on them: 56–60%
     right, with 41% chance. The 27B gets 85%.
   - Only rows with list/fetch decoys teach it (`dtw25_RD` 77%, SPt with 50%
     S6 87%).
   - Those rows still damage lookalike choices. The reader halves the
     damage but doesn't remove it: `dtw25_RD` is right on 71.5% of
     lookalikes and scores 63.3% on plain, against R19's A0 on the same rows
     at 62.1% and 53.9%.
   - The owner ruled out the 27B-label route for now. So this is the one gap
     left, and it needs a way to teach that sense that doesn't touch
     lookalike choices.

## Terms

- **Reader:** Ternlight mini (9.5M parameters, ternary weights, 5 MB), frozen.
  - Each tool description is encoded once, in `reader.pt`
    (`models/tiny/reader_table.py`); the request is encoded per task.
  - The planner (`train.py --reader`) gets three things from it, about 197k
    new parameters:
    - the tool's reader vector, added to its line vector;
    - the request's vector, as one more memory row;
    - the request–tool cosine, added to the tool pointer's score with a
      weight the decoder sets per slot.
- **Corpora:** each 30,000 rows, 25% new plus 75% options-off, as R17 and
  R19.
  - `s6off`: options off.
  - `dtw25`: 25% decoys on every tool plus twin roles (R19).
  - `fdc25`: 25% decoys only on the four flip slots, and only where the
    request decides (`data.gen --decoy-slots flip`), with twin roles.
- **Decision classes and swaps:** as R20 and R21. Tool-choice rates are on the
  300-task samples (`llm_baseline.py`, `reader_bakeoff.py`); exam scores on
  the full exams.

## What this means

- **The architecture question is answered for reading.** A frozen 9.5M
  ternary encoder plus a 9.6M planner beats every earlier tiny model on the
  exams that need reading, and costs nothing on the plain one. The reader's
  work on tool descriptions happens once per tool; per task it reads only
  the request, about 3 ms on this CPU as WASM.
- **The corpus lesson from R19 holds.** Decoys the request decides help once
  a reader is there. Decoys it can't decide still cost lookalike choices.
- **The best model now:** `models/tiny/runs/pod_rd/runs/fdc25_RD/best.pt` on
  `data_cache_fdc25`, with `--backoff 3` for the plain exam.

## Next (for the owner)

1. **The "normal tool" sense without lookalike damage,** from rows only:
   - add list/fetch decoy rows only where the task has no lookalike pair,
     so they can't teach the planner to ignore the request between two
     things; or
   - teach it through a small separate head on the reader's tool vectors,
     trained on list/fetch decoy decisions, which leaves the pointer alone.

   Either is one more pod run of about $1.
2. **A second seed of `fdc25_RD`,** before quoting its plain score as a gain.
   79.5% equals the best, and single-seed plain differences under about 2.5
   points are noise. The decoy and flip gains (+10 to +17) are far outside
   it.
3. **Phase 3, the NPU:** export the reader and planner as separate
   fixed-shape stages and time them on Strix Point. Kernel work, separately
   approved.

## Caveats

- **One seed per run.**
- **Ternlight runs as a frozen feature extractor.** Its 30,522-word table
  (four fifths of its size) stays as it ships. A smaller table means
  redistilling our own ternary reader with its recipe.
- **The flip probe's tasks come from the same 42 unseen worlds as the
  exams;** only its decoy wiring differs.
- **The 300-task samples used for tool choice show the same ordering as the
  full exams,** but their goal columns differ from the full-exam ones by a
  few points.
