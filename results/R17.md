# R17: a quarter of S6's rows costs most of the plain score and buys all of the flip skill

**Date:** 2026-09-26/27. **Branch:** `description-reading`.
**Plan:** `.claude/plans/decoy-fraction.md` (revised before any spend).
**Spend:** $0.77: one secure-cloud A40 at $0.49/hr, three runs side by side.
The balance went from $12.82 to $12.05.
**Everything the pod wrote is on the laptop** in
`models/tiny/runs/pod_s6frac/`. All 34 files were checked against the pod's
listing by size before the pod was deleted.

## The result

1. **The generator-drift worry is closed without training.** Today's
   generator reproduces S6's training rows exactly: all 28,739 rows match
   `data/s6_shards/train_*.jsonl`, and the combined files share an MD5
   (`results/logs/gen_s6today.sh`). R14's caveat 2 was wrong and is
   corrected there. The 32-point cost R14 measured is the three options, not
   a change in the generator.
2. **There is no cheap middle.** Putting S6's options back on a quarter of
   the rows costs almost as much on the plain exam as putting them on all of
   them:

   | S6 rows in training | A0 plain | SPt plain | SPt decoyed | SPt flip |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 0% (R14, R16) | 79.5% | 72.8% | 46.2% | 24.6% |
   | **25%** | **54.4%** | **61.7%** | 49.6% | **38.8%** |
   | **50%** | — | 60.0% | 50.9% | 40.5% |
   | 100% (R13) | 47.1% | 58.6% | 50.4% | 38.8% |

   - **A0:** a quarter of S6's rows costs 25 of the 32 points.
   - **SPt:** it costs 11 of 14.
   - **What SPt gets in return:** at 25% it already has all of the flip
     skill (38.8%, the same as with S6 on every row) and most of the decoy
     gain.
   - **Going to 50%:** changes every number by under 2 points.
3. **The cost is R15's same-shape swap, spread to almost every world.** The
   swap is calling another entity's getter or lister of the same form. As a
   share of plain-exam tasks:

   | model | S6 rows | swaps, R15's seven worlds | swaps, other 35 worlds | swaps that compile |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | A0 | 0% | 34.5% | 0.6% | 4 of 141 |
   | A0 | 25% | 41.3% | **31.0%** | 19 of 659 |
   | SPt | 25% | 26.3% | 23.5% | 27 of 481 |

   With no S6 rows, A0's swaps were a quirk of seven worlds (7 of the 42
   worlds at 10% or more of their tasks, median 0%). With a quarter of S6's
   rows, 29 of the 42 are at 10% or more, and the median world is at 30.8%.
   For SPt at 25% it is 28 worlds, with a median of 19.6%. They still almost never compile. So
   the planner calls a tool whose parameter types do not fit the arguments it
   then passes, which the input shows it.
4. **Tool backoff recovers part of it, and loses nothing anywhere:**

   | model | S6 rows | exam | greedy | `--backoff 3` | gained / lost |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | A0 | 25% | plain | 54.4% | 65.3% | +219 / −0 |
   | SPt | 25% | plain | 61.7% | 68.0% | +126 / −0 |
   | SPt | 50% | plain | 60.0% | 64.6% | +93 / −0 |
   | SPt | 25% | decoyed | 49.6% | **53.4%** | +76 / −0 |
   | SPt | 50% | decoyed | 50.9% | **54.1%** | +64 / −0 |

   **54.1% is the best decoyed-exam score so far.** The previous best was
   R13's SPt at 51.7% with best-of-4.
5. **No single model is best on both exams:**
   - **Plain exam:** A0 on the options-off rows with backoff, at 84.5%
     (R15). Every S6 row it trains on costs it.
   - **Decoyed exam and flip probe:** SPt with a quarter to half S6 rows,
     at 53–54% decoyed with backoff and 39–41% flip. It pays 11–13 points
     of plain score against its own options-off version.

## Why decoy training would cause this: a hypothesis, not tested

Every options-off row is signature-unique: no two tools on a row share
parameter and return types (`gen_s6off.sh` needs `--allow-signature-unique`
for that reason). So a planner trained only on those rows can pick a tool by
its types. The swapped getters and listers differ in exactly that: the
advertiser getter takes `I:advertiser`, the spot getter takes `I:spot`, and
the input says `I0 ID:spot`.

S6's rows give every mutating tool one or two decoys with the same types, so
on those rows types never decide. A planner that learns from any of those
rows that types do not decide has lost the one thing that separated the
getters.

Three observations fit this:

- the swaps are type errors (96% do not compile)
- a quarter of the rows is enough, as a lesson learned rather than a
  proportion would be
- more S6 rows add little beyond it

Two things remain untested:

- The three options still move together here. Opaque names and twin roles
  could matter as well as decoys.
- Nothing yet shows the planner ignoring types in some direct way, other
  than through the compile failures.

This is also the memory note "signature shortcut defect" seen from the
other side. The plain exam can be passed by matching types. A0's high
plain scores lean on that, and decoy training takes it away.

## Other numbers

| model | S6 rows | flip | test (training worlds) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A0 | 25% | 25.4% | 90.5% |
| SPt | 25% | 38.8% | 90.1% |
| SPt | 50% | 40.5% | 93.4% |

A0 at 25% scores 40.5% on the decoyed exam, against 37.4% with no S6 rows
and 38.6% with all of them. As in R14, decoy training does little for A0 on
the exam it was built for.

The test split mixes decoyed and plain rows in the same proportion as the
training rows, so it is not comparable across the 0%, 25% and 50% columns.

## What this changes

- **For the plain exam, the options-off rows stay the corpus.** No fraction
  of S6's rows is free.
- **The next split is by option, at 25%, since 25% is enough to show the
  effect.** Three arms, each on 25% of rows with the rest options-off:
  1. decoys only (`--decoys 1:2`, no twin roles, no opaque names)
  2. decoys with twin roles (no opaque names)
  3. opaque names only (`--decoys 0 --opaque-names 0.15`)

  If the swaps come with arm 1, the type-matching hypothesis gains; if
  with arm 3, it loses. Each arm needs about an hour of laptop generation;
  the pod run for all three is about $0.80. This needs a go-ahead.
- **A training fix to try after that, if decoys are the cause:** keep the
  getters and listers type-distinct on decoyed rows, or penalise
  type-inconsistent calls during training. Neither is designed yet.
