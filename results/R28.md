**Terms.** *Left to right*: today's decoder, one program slot at a time, each
slot seeing the ones before it. *Draft stage*: a diffusion stage that fills
all 64 slots at once from a blank canvas, looped with the same weights.
*Refiner*: a left-to-right stage that reads the context and the draft.
*Loads*: weight loads per program on the NPU (encoder counts as one).
*Early exit*: skip the refiner when the draft's lowest slot confidence clears
a threshold. *Arms*: `base` left to right, 4 layers; `ctrl6` left to right, 6
layers (the parameter control); `draft` one draft stage, 4 layers x 4 loops,
no refiner; `DR` draft 4x4 then refiner 2x1 (the design); `D2R` two draft
stages 2x4 then refiner 2x1; `DRx` DR trained with mistakes put into its
drafts (added mid-run, below); `DRxf` DR whose refiner trained on
cross-fitted drafts (step 2b, point 10); `DRc` / `D2Rc` DR / D2R whose draft
commits its surest slots between loops (step 2c, point 12); `DRp` / `D2Rp`
DR / D2R with a constant picker (step 2d, point 13). All on the D3 corpus,
two seeds each.

   Counting layer runs that must happen one after another, from the real

## What this means

- Per step 2's own gate (before the picker), the draft added nothing a
  deeper left-to-right decoder doesn't on accuracy, and ctrl6 (6 layers)
  was the best or tied-best arm on test, decoy, flip and cluttered.
- That gate did not count speed (point 9). On plain and the held-out worlds
  the staged decoder matches left to right at a third of the steps in a row.
  The owner made the gate progress instead (point 10).
- The seen-world gap was decoy constants (points 11, 13): the draft, and the
  refiner after it, pointed at constants the request never asked for. Three
  fixes aimed at the draft or at what the refiner reads missed (DRx, DRxf,
  DRc/D2Rc). A constant picker trained with the decoder closes most of the
  gap (point 14): DR 64.9 -> DRp 81.8 on test, ctrl6 84.0, with constant
  slots as accurate as ctrl6's.
- With the picker, the staged decoder is 2-3 points behind ctrl6 on seen
  worlds, 2-4 points ahead on plain and cluttered, level on decoy and 3-4
  behind on flip, at under a third of ctrl6's steps in a row. On seen
  worlds what is left is observe-act tool, field and keyword choices, not
  constants.
- Still open: DRp's house collapse (picker or noise, two seeds can't say);
  the early exit on new worlds (one D2Rp seed loses 6 points with it); and
  the speed itself, which is modelled, not timed on an NPU.
- Experts (step 3) were designed for the staged decoder. They could sit on
  left to right instead, but D3 removed their strongest motive (forgetting),
  and the router question is trivial for today's three types. The natural
  time to revisit them is the first real bolt-on (report or scheduling).

### Recipe and deviations from the plan

- R10's recipe: left-to-right training, 12 epochs, batch 64, PAD weight 0.5,
  packed rows, width 256. No reader: the owner removed Ternlight
  (2026-10-02). The encoder is today's, trained with each arm.
- Staged arms train the draft (all 64 slots, from a blank canvas, PAD slots
  at weight 0.5) and the refiner (left to right, reading the draft as extra
  context rows: slot embedding + position + confidence) together, with 10%
  of draft slots randomized for the refiner (5% plus 25% same-kind swaps in
  DRx). The plan's frozen-draft training (`--stage-train`) was not built.
- The router ran after step 2, on step 2's trained encoders, not before.
- DRx was added after the first results showed the copying.
- D3 = the old corpus plus all 9,034 new rows: 36,000 training rows
  (data 28,935, observe-act 5,385, pages 1,680).
- Cost: about $6 against the plan's $3: 14 runs instead of 10, and staged
  runs took about 93 minutes each.
- Step 2b, 2c and 2d were added by the owner after step 2 (plan sections
  2b-2d); each pod run had its own go-ahead.
