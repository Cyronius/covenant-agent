# R3 — the tiny planner, started: a 7.9M model that cannot bind a symbol to its line

**Status:** in progress. Two runs, one seed each, one model size. The R3 pass
bar has not been attempted, because no model has cleared chance on tool
selection yet. The reason is diagnosed (section 3) and is the subject of the
next round. Nothing here triggers R3's fail action.

**Date:** 2026-09-17, both runs and the diagnosis. **Code:** `models/tiny/`,
moved from `abc-diffusion-window/canvas/` (its commit `0d9d1c2`) the same day.
**Plans:** `.claude/plans/r3-canvas-experiment.md` (the original design),
`.claude/plans/r3-round3-symbol-binding.md` (next round). **Model:** a
7.9M-parameter encoder-decoder, from scratch: 3 encoder layers over the
serialized context, 4 decoder layers over a 64-slot output canvas, width 256.
Two arms differ in one line, whether decoder self-attention is causal. The
causal arm is an ordinary left-to-right control; the other is a masked-diffusion
generator that fills every slot in parallel. **Data:** `data/s5_plain.jsonl`,
all 30,000 tasks, of which 25,463 single-segment programs of at most 60 tokens
were used; 90/5/5 split by task, plus one whole world held out. **Pod:** RunPod
RTX 4090, 1.34 hours across both runs, about $1. **Scored:** every generated
program compiled by `core.pipeline.build` and executed by `harness.run.run_task`
against world state. No number below comes from any other path.

## 0. How this relates to R3 as written in `PLAN.md` §5

Each deviation is deliberate and is meant to be closed, not kept.

- **Encoder-decoder, not decoder-only.** The input runs about 1,100 tokens and
  the output 30 at the median, so encoding once and decoding against a fixed
  memory is the cheap shape, and the diffusion arm needs a fixed memory to
  cross-attend to. Both arms share the encoder, so their comparison is
  decoder-side only.
- **25k pairs and 6 epochs, not a million pairs.** About 2,400 steps. The training curves came back with the checkpoints and are in `results/logs/r3/curves/`. The control's validation loss fell from 0.80 to 0.36 and was flat for the last third of the run (0.372 to 0.362 over the final 1,200 steps) under a cosine schedule that decays to zero, so more of the same schedule would have bought little. The diffusion arm's was still falling (0.330 to 0.272). Neither curve says anything about symbol-slot accuracy, which is why the next round logs it directly.
  The next round adopts R3's data budget.
- **One size, not the curve.** The size curve is meaningless until one size is
  above chance on tool selection.
- **No constrained decoding yet.** The run 2 pointer head masked undeclared
  symbols, which is C4 restricted to symbols; the unmasked baseline already
  respected that constraint 99.87% of the time on its own.
- **The diffusion arm is §12 material.** Diffusion-style editing is deferred
  until R3 and R4 produce a model worth optimizing. It rides along here because
  its one-pass speed result (section 1) is why the experiment was built, and
  because the two arms cost the same to train. Its quality comparison is
  unreadable until a model can do the task, which is §12's rule found
  empirically.
- **The bar.** `results/R2.md` fixes R3's target: the 0.8B condition B model
  posts 100% on levels 0 to 6. R3 passes when a model at or under 40M reaches
  that under C4.

## 1. Run 1: both arms fail the same way

One seed, 6 epochs, 1,415 test tasks, 45 minutes of GPU.

| arm | decoder passes | parse | compile | goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control (left to right) | 29.1 | 100% | 8.6% | 8.3% |
| diffusion, 1 step | 1.0 | 75.5% | 8.3% | 7.5% |
| diffusion, 2 steps | 2.0 | 74.7% | 7.7% | 7.2% |
| diffusion, 4 steps | 4.0 | 77.4% | 7.3% | 7.0% |
| diffusion, 8 steps | 8.0 | 80.1% | 7.3% | 7.1% |
| diffusion, 16 steps | 16.0 | 85.5% | 7.6% | 7.1% |
| diffusion, 32 steps | 32.0 | 89.9% | 7.6% | 7.1% |

Passes are decoder passes; both arms encode the input once. On the held-out
world: control 8.7% goal, diffusion at 8 steps 9.1%. Both arms score slightly
better on tools they have never seen, which already said that tool identity was
not being used.

**What the model learned and did not.** Control programs parse 100% of the
time, put `FOREACH`, `IF` and `FILTER` in the right places, and get the abort
tasks right when no symbol is needed. Every level that requires naming a tool
scores 0 to 3%. Level 16 (`ABORT UNSUPPORTED`, a four-way classification of the
request) scores 99%. Level 11 (`NOT_FOUND`) scores 33%, and 35% of its
references are the bare abort with no symbols; the rest need a lookup and fail
like everything else. A typical failure, structure exactly right and every
symbol wrong:

```
GEN: CALL T1  I0 -> r0 / FORMAT S0 r0.F12 r0.F12 -> r1 / CALL T15 r0.F9  r1 / STOP
REF: CALL T2  I0 -> r0 / FORMAT S0 r0.F10 r0.F16 -> r1 / CALL T10 r0.F11 r1 / STOP
```

Diagnostics over 1,415 control programs: 2,200 `TYPE_ERROR`, 1,660
`MISSING_ARG`, 1,056 `EFFECT_UNDECLARED`, 889 `UNBOUND`, 345 `UNKNOWN_FIELD`.
Wrong tool, so wrong arity, so wrong types, so wrong effects header.

**Three secondary results, all taken at the floor.**

- One diffusion pass reaches 90% of the control's goal success for a
  twenty-ninth of the decoder work. Steps buy syntax, not semantics: parse
  climbs monotonically from 75% to 90% across the sweep while goal success stays
  at 7.1%.
- The compiler in the sampling loop (remask the line a diagnostic blames,
  refill, up to 2 rounds) raised parse by 5.8 points and goal by 0.3. Repair
  fired on 1,312 of 1,415 programs and 5 of those ended up compiling. A critic
  can detect a wrong symbol; remasking the line regenerates the same wrong
  symbol.
- The targets carry a derived `EFFECTS` header, the union of the effects of the
  tools called below it, planted so the first line depends on the rest. Among
  parsed programs the control got it wrong 64% of the time and diffusion 68 to
  71%. The dependency planted to favour diffusion went the other way, most
  likely because the header and the calls are filled from independent
  marginals at nearby steps. Confounded by the grounding failure; provisional.

## 2. Run 2: a pointer head is a null, and two ablations

Run 1 read the failure as "symbols are per-request, so an embedding has nothing
stable to learn," and the fix was a pointer head: score each canvas slot
against the encoder state at the symbol's declaration line and emit the winner,
with undeclared symbols masked out. Verified before training that every
reference symbol was locatable (9,575 of 9,575).

| model | arm | passes | compile | goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | control | 29.1 | 8.6% | 8.3% |
| pointer | control | 29.1 | 8.2% | 7.7% |
| base | diffusion, 1 step | 1.0 | 8.3% | 7.5% |
| pointer | diffusion, 1 step | 1.0 | 8.5% | 7.5% |
| base | diffusion, 8 steps | 8.0 | 7.3% | 7.1% |
| pointer | diffusion, 8 steps | 8.0 | 7.8% | 6.9% |

On the held-out world the pointer is worse: 4.7% against 8.7% for the control.

Per-symbol accuracy at reference positions, base control:

| symbol kind | candidates per task | chance | model |
|---|---|---|---|
| tool | 17.9 | 5.6% | 6% |
| field | 20.9 | 4.8% | 5% |
| string constant | 4.3 | 23% | 95% |
| ID constant | 1.1 | 90% | 96% |

Two inference-only ablations on 400 test tasks, no retraining:

| input change | programs byte-identical |
|---|---|
| tool descriptions permuted among the tool lines | 93.0% |
| request replaced with another task's | 0.8% |

Run 2 concluded that the model reads the request, ignores the descriptions,
and picks tools by type signature, "which cannot disambiguate eighteen tools."
It recommended a pretrained text encoder first. Section 3 shows both halves of
that conclusion were wrong.

## 3. The diagnosis: the corpus never needed the descriptions, and the model is not reading signatures either

Run 2's own last recommendation was to check whether the corpus rewards reading
descriptions before spending on anything. Done on the laptop against the run 1
generations, no GPU. `models/tiny/diagnose.py` reproduces every number.

**The signature identifies the tool.** A typed tool line is
`T8 (I:policyholder=F2 S=F17) -> - [SEND] :: Place a call to a policyholder.`
Over the 2,696 reference `CALL`s in the test split:

| how much of the signature is used | called tool unique in its task |
|---|---|
| full signature, field symbols included | 100.0% |
| types and effects only, field symbols stripped | 93.3% |
| field symbols replaced by the field names | 100.0% |

A model that read only the signature would score about 93% on tool selection.
The description is never needed to choose a tool. Fields are different: only
55.9% of referenced fields are unique by entity and type, so field choice needs
the name after `::`, `claim.status` against `claim.image`.

**The model is not reading signatures.** For each reference `CALL`, the tool the
model wrote on the same line. Chance is exact: a uniform pick over the task's
declared tools.

| | control | diffusion, 8 steps | chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| same tool | 6.1% | 5.6% | 5.6% |
| same effect class (READ, WRITE, SEND...) | 27.5% | 26.6% | 27.2% |
| same parameter count | 27.8% | 26.3% | 27.1% |

A model that knew only "this request needs a SEND tool" would be far above
chance on the effect row. Both arms are at chance to the first decimal. The
model does not know which line `T8` is on. That is the whole failure, and it is
symbol binding, not English.

**Why binding failed, mechanically.**

- The input tokenizer (byte-pair, 4,096 entries) splits every symbol. `T8` is
  `T` then `8`; `T23` is `T` `2` `3`. The `T` at the start of every tool line is
  the same token on all eighteen lines and the digits are the same tokens as
  every other number in the input. The run 2 pointer head pointed at that bare
  `T`, the least informative token on the line.
- Nothing connects an input symbol to the output symbol of the same name. The
  output vocabulary has `T23` as one token with its own embedding. Copying a
  symbol from input to canvas is an arbitrary mapping to learn, forty times
  over, from 2,400 steps.
- The output vocabulary holds `r0.F6` as one token: 115 of its 500 entries are
  register-dot-field compounds, and 708 of the 2,064 field references in the
  test references use that form. A field symbol inside a compound is not a
  symbol slot at all to the output head, so nothing that ties or points at
  `F6` can reach a third of the field references until the output side
  splits them.
- The curves (section 0) show the control converged under its schedule while
  still at chance on symbols, so "train longer" alone is not the likely fix for
  it; the diffusion arm was still improving and gets that check.

**Corrections to the run write-ups.** Level 11 scores 33%, not the 99 to 100%
run 1 stated; that is its bare-abort share. Run 2's "chooses by signature"
mechanism is contradicted by the effect-class row. The pretrained encoder
recommendation is withdrawn: it solves a problem the corpus does not pose.

## 4. Pass or fail

Neither. R3's pass bar (a model at or under 40M reaching 100% on levels 0 to 6
under C4) has not been attempted. R3's fail action (no model at or under 150M
reaches R2's level) is not triggered: one size was trained, its failure is at
chance on a sub-problem that signature reading alone would take to 93%, and the
causes found are representational, not capacity.

## 5. What changed in the plan

- R3 is amended (`PLAN.md` §14, 2026-09-17) to admit the encoder-decoder shape
  and to record the bar and the deviations above.
- The next round is representation-first: atomic symbol tokens in the input
  tokenizer, a shared symbol embedding tying input symbols to output symbols,
  the pointer re-pointed at the real symbol token, and symbol-slot accuracy
  logged during training so undertrained and unable can be told apart on the
  curve. Four models differing by one thing each, 12 epochs, about an hour of
  GPU. Then the control arm with the winner. Gate to the size curve and to any
  diffusion comparison: tool-slot accuracy at or above 60% and compile at or
  above 40%. Plan: `.claude/plans/r3-round3-symbol-binding.md`.
- C4 for the tiny sampler reuses `harness/task_grammar.py`, which already
  enumerates exactly the declared symbols per task, instead of the pointer's
  masking.
- The data budget moves toward R3's: `s5_plain` and `s5_crowded` together are
  56,000 tasks, and the generator makes more. Crowded contexts (15 to 60 tools)
  will need the 1,280-token input cap raised; `prep.py` fails rather than
  truncates, by design.
- A pretrained encoder is off the table until a model binds symbols on the test
  split and fails on the held-out world, which would say the request-to-signature
  hop does not generalize across worlds.
- Pod hygiene, now standing rules after two losses in run 2: upload to a staging
  path and rename into place; never download a file a job is still writing;
  copy every `runs/*/log.jsonl` into `results/logs/r3/curves/` and read it
  before writing up. Runs 1 and 2 brought theirs back and nobody looked.
