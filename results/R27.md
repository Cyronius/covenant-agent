# R27: ELECTRA alone reads short texts nearly as well as its teacher, and one pass is enough

**Date:** 2026-10-02. **Branch:** `description-reading`. **Spend:** about
$0.45 (community RTX 3090, $0.22/hr, about 2 hours including a restart).
**Plan:** `.claude/plans/electra-only-reader.md`, step 0. The owner dropped
Ternlight from the reader ("remove anything that follows ternlight") and asked
why ELECTRA should run twice; this step answers both.

**Terms.** *Reader*: what turns each text (request, constant, field, tool
description) into vectors for the planner. *Teacher*: all-MiniLM-L6-v2, whose
vectors the reader learns to copy. *Head*: the small network on top of the
frozen ternary ELECTRA (R26's `e1`) that turns a stretch of words into one
vector. *Read alone*: ELECTRA reads a piece (a role's words, a 4-word chunk)
by itself. *One pass*: ELECTRA reads the whole request once and the head pools
each piece's words out of that pass. *Constant probe*: whether the request's
vectors score the constants the reference program uses above the ones it
doesn't (pair accuracy, 50 = chance; cluttered / plain / demo).

## The result

1. **One pass reads as well as reading each piece alone.** Head *a* was
   trained reading every piece alone; head *b* on half its pieces pooled out
   of one pass. Same pieces, targets and steps.

   | constant probe | 4-word chunks | slots + chunks |
   |---|---|---|
   | a, read alone | 84.3 / 59.6 / 85.5 | 83.8 / 61.2 / 84.8 |
   | **b, one pass** | **83.5 / 59.0 / 86.5** | **83.4 / 60.6 / 84.8** |
   | a, one pass (never trained on it) | 84.4 / 57.4 / 87.8 | 84.5 / 58.5 / 87.8 |

   b in one pass is within a point of a read alone on every suite, so the
   reader can run ELECTRA once per request. The step's gate for choosing b
   passed.

2. **It misses its teacher by 3 points on the plain suite, and the miss is
   almost all names.** Gate: a's chunks within 2 points of the teacher's on
   every suite. Missed on plain (59.6 vs 62.8) and demo (85.5 vs 87.8; the
   demo is 10 tasks, so one pair moves it).

   | 4-word chunks | cluttered / plain / demo |
   |---|---|
   | word overlap, no model | 83.1 / 64.6 / 92.2 |
   | teacher | 84.8 / 62.8 / 87.8 |
   | a, read alone | 84.3 / 59.6 / 85.5 |
   | R26's reader (Ternlight; dropped) | 87.1 / 66.6 / 84.8 |

   On the plain suite, by the kind of constant the program uses (pairs):

   | | ID (1,072) | text (1,101) | enum (318) | time (154) | true/false (312) |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | word overlap | 84.2 | 61.4 | 70.0 | 88.3 | 0.0 |
   | teacher | 89.0 | 57.7 | 45.6 | 90.9 | 1.9 |
   | a, read alone | **78.5** | 59.1 | 44.3 | 92.2 | 3.5 |
   | R26's reader | 92.4 | 62.4 | 47.2 | 92.2 | 8.7 |

   ID constants are record titles and people: "The Understory", "Cell
   viability assay", "Elif Kerr's instructor id", "Umbrella's id". ELECTRA's
   head is 10 points behind the teacher there and level or ahead on
   everything else. Its training pieces were general English plus WordNet
   lemmas and API names: almost no people's names, company names or titled
   records.

3. **The teacher itself is the ceiling.** On the plain suite, MiniLM is below
   plain word overlap (62.8 vs 64.6), and the dropped Ternlight was above both
   (66.6). Copying MiniLM perfectly would still leave the reader 4 points under
   the old one on plain.

4. **The head leans on ELECTRA's lowest layers.** Both heads put about 56% of
   their layer mix on the embeddings and layers 1-3, which carry the word
   itself more than its sentence. That's likely why reading in one pass costs
   so little.

Held-out general text: cosine to the teacher 0.885 (a, alone), 0.893 (b, one
pass); pairs ranked in the teacher's order 0.874 / 0.870.

## Follow-up: names in the head's pieces (head n, 2026-10-03, about $0.20)

The owner chose the first option above. `electra_reader.py targets
--names-extra 170000 --sub-rows 40000` generated name-like texts from Faker
(22 Latin-script locales, MIT): 51,000 people, 17,000 first or last names,
25,500 companies, 42,500 "<person or company>'s <word> id", 25,500 titles and
8,500 numbered records. It also made 40,000 general texts with one object,
destination or source span replaced by such a name. A name was dropped if it
shared a word with any capitalized word of the probe suites' ID constants or
the generator's own lists (528 words: "acme", "ada", "adler", "alvarez", ...).
Head n is head b retrained with a quarter of every batch drawn from these
(`run_e0n.sh`, RTX A4000).

| constant probe, 4-word chunks | cluttered / plain / demo |
|---|---|
| teacher | 84.8 / 62.8 / 87.8 |
| b, one pass (R27) | 83.5 / 59.0 / 86.5 |
| **n, one pass** | **84.2 / 60.2 / 85.1** |
| n, read alone | 84.6 / 60.4 / 85.8 |

On the plain suite, by the kind of ID constant the program uses (pairs):

| | proper name (246) | reference: "X's thing id" (458) | title (368) |
|---|---|---|---|
| word overlap | 100.0 | 63.1 | 100.0 |
| teacher | 99.2 | 76.2 | 98.4 |
| b, one pass | 97.2 | 52.2 | 100.0 |
| **n, one pass** | **99.2** | **60.7** | 99.7 |

- **Proper names are fixed:** n matches the teacher (99.2).
- **References are half fixed:** 52 → 61, against the teacher's 76. The
  generated references used random WordNet words as the thing ("Jane Vũ's
  lumper id"); the exam's are person roles ("Lena Adler's grower id") that
  the request names ("text the grower of it"). The teacher matches the role
  word; n still leans on the name.
- Held-out generated names: cosine to the teacher 0.787 → 0.846 (one pass).
  General text is unchanged (0.893 → 0.888).
- **Gate:** still missed, now narrowly: plain 60.2 against the needed 60.8,
  demo 85.1 against 85.8 (10 tasks).

## How it ran

`run_e0.sh` on the pod: MiniLM embedded 1,354,541 texts (200,000 general
texts' pieces and 100,000 names) in 76 s; each head trained 6,000 steps
(batches of 512 pieces read alone plus 128 texts' pieces, alone or one pass);
then `electra_reader.py check`. The two heads side by side needed about 23 GB
and head a died of OOM on the 24 GB card; it reran after b
(`e0_resume.sh`), and `run_e0.sh` now trains them one after the other.
