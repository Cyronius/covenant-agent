# R21: a 9.5M ternary encoder reads decoys as well as the 27B, without any training on our tasks

**Date:** 2026-09-28. **Branch:** `description-reading`. **Spend:** $0
(laptop CPU). **Plan:** `.claude/plans/npu-planner.md`, phase 1, Ternlight
first at the owner's request ("let's try ternary first and see how it
does").

## The result

1. **Ternlight reads the decoys the request decides as well as anything we
   have measured, except the 27B.**
   - Ternlight mini, 9.5M parameters, ternary weights, 5 MB: 88.9% zero-shot,
     with no training on our tasks.
   - The 27B: 98%. SPt after training on 50% S6 rows: 91%. A0: 45%, below
     chance (41%).
   - Ternlight base (15.4M): 90.5%. bge-small (33M, full precision): 91.1%.
2. **Ternary costs almost nothing here.** Ternlight was distilled from
   all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (22.7M, full precision), which scores 86.6% on the same
   decisions. Its ternary students match or beat it.
3. **No small reader has the 27B's sense of which tool is the normal one.** On
   decoys the request doesn't decide, every reader is at 47–54%, against 41%
   chance. The 27B is at 85%.
   - SPt trained on 50% S6 rows learned that sense: 87%. So it is learnable
     from rows.
   - It can also be taught from the 27B's own picks.
4. **Lookalikes are not a reading problem.**
   - Readers score 68–77%; A0 88%, the 27B 97%.
   - These requests usually name both things ("notify the advertisers with a
     live-read spot"). One similarity score over the whole request can't say
     which one a given step fetches. The planner decides that from the
     program's structure, and backoff fixes most of what it misses (R15, R20:
     A0 + backoff swaps 0.3%).
5. **So the parts exist, each at a different piece.** A planner that combines
   A0's structure (lookalikes), a small reader's similarity (decidable
   decoys) and a learned prior (undecidable decoys) could approach the 27B on
   all three. That is phase 2.

## Terms

- **Decision:** one tool the reference calls, with its rivals present in the
  task. Rivals are same-shape tools of another thing (lookalike) or
  same-signature siblings (decoy). The candidates average 2.0 (lookalike) and
  2.6 (decoy), so chance is 50% and 41%.
- **Reader, zero-shot:** encode the request and each candidate's
  description; pick the closest. "+name" adds the tool's name to its text.
  - Ternlight runs through its npm package (WASM).
  - The references run through `transformers`, pooled as their model cards
    say.
- **Generators (27B, A0, SPt):** scored from their programs on the same
  decisions. They count as right or wrong only when they called exactly one
  side: the right tool and no rival, or a rival and not the right tool. The
  "committed" count says how often that happened.
- **Decision classes:** as R20. "Unlabelled" decoys are on rows whose tool
  names don't match the theme (meaningless-name rows), so decidability isn't
  known.

**Speed on this laptop's CPU,** single-threaded WASM, one text at a time:
Ternlight base 5.5 ms per text, mini 2.8 ms.

**Size:**

- About three quarters of each Ternlight model is its 30,522-word table
  (4-bit): mini 7.8M of 9.5M, base 11.7M of 15.4M.
- The two ternary transformer layers are only about 2–4M.
- A pruned or 4,096-piece vocabulary would shrink the table severalfold.
  That would need retraining the table, which Ternlight's published format
  doesn't support directly.

## What this means for the plan

- **Ternary is viable for the reader.** On the decisions a reader can make at
  all, Ternlight's ternary weights lose nothing to the full-precision
  teacher.
- **Phase 2 should use Ternlight mini as the frozen reader.**
  - Tool descriptions are encoded once and stored.
  - The request is encoded per task, at about 3 ms on the CPU today.
  - A0's planner gets a reading term in its tool pointer: similarity between
    request and tool.
  - The "normal tool" prior is learned from rows that carry it. Those are the
    decoy rows SPt learned it from, which R19 found also damaged lookalikes.
    Or it is learned from the 27B's picks on undecidable decisions, as soft
    labels (a Cerebras run of a few dollars).
- **Readers not yet scored:** arctic-embed-xs, MiniLM-L3, BERT-mini/tiny, a
  static-vector floor. Ternlight already matches the 33M bge-small on the
  decisions that matter, so these are lower priority.

## Caveats

- **Zero-shot similarity is a floor, not a ceiling.** A reader trained or
  fine-tuned inside the planner can do better, and the planner decides how
  much to trust it.
- **The Ternlight weights ship only inside WASM and a custom format.** Phase 2
  runs it as a frozen feature extractor through node. Fine-tuning it, or
  giving it a smaller word table, means re-implementing its format or
  redistilling our own ternary reader with its recipe. R12's stages are
  close to that already.
- **Generator numbers are on committed decisions only,** so they are not
  exactly comparable with a reader's forced choice. The 27B's no-commit cases
  are mostly different program shapes (R20).
