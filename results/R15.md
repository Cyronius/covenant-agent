# R15: the leftover "wrong entity" failure is a decoding problem

**Date:** 2026-09-26. **Branch:** `description-reading`.
**Spend:** $0. Everything ran on the laptop against R14's checkpoints.
**Picks up:** R14 "Next runs" item 3, the residual wrong-entity failure.

## The result

1. **R14's 9.8% "wrong entity's tool" is two different failures.**
   - **7.0% of tasks call another entity's tool of the same shape.** The
     planner calls the parent entity's getter or lister where the child's was
     needed: the traveler's instead of the case's, the creator's instead of the
     issue's, the repo's instead of the build's. Everything else in the program
     is usually right, including the fields it then reads. **137 of these 141
     programs do not compile.** An example is `CALL T8 I0`, which passes a spot
     id to the advertiser getter, while the input lists `I0 ID:spot`.
   - **2.7% of tasks pick the right entity but the wrong action.** Examples are
     adding a note instead of editing one, or setting a status instead of
     flagging. 41 of these 54 compile. These are real description-reading
     errors. 13 of them are L9's deliberately vague requests ("Sort out the
     stale drafts").
2. **The same-shape swap is concentrated in seven worlds.** Seven of the 42
   exam worlds hold 131 of the 141 cases, at 20–47% of their tasks. The other
   35 worlds fail it on 0.6% of theirs. Run 1 (A0 trained on S5's rows) fails the same seven worlds at about
   the same rates. SPt mostly does not: ci_cd is 63% for run 1 against 8% for
   run 2. The cause is not how alike the tools' descriptions are, and not how
   the tokenizer splits the entity names (see "Why these seven worlds").
3. **A decoding fix recovers most of it without retraining.** "Tool backoff":
   decode greedily, and if the program fails to compile, put the model's
   runner-up tool at a CALL slot and decode greedily again from there. It is
   capped at three extra decodes, the same budget as `evaluate.py --best-of 4`.

   | decoding | S6 plain | the seven worlds | the other 35 |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | greedy (R14) | 79.5% | 55.5% | 85.1% |
   | best-of-4 (resample at temperature 0.7) | 81.2% | 63.2% | 85.4% |
   | **tool backoff** | **84.5%** | **78.9%** | 85.8% |

   Backoff gains 101 tasks and loses none. Best-of-4 gains 35 and loses none.
4. **It holds on data it was not designed on.** Both checks below are on
   programs the pattern was not found in:

   | model | exam | greedy | tool backoff | tasks gained / lost |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | run 2: SPt, S5 rows | S6 plain | 71.7% | **75.5%** | +77 / −0 |
   | run 3: A0, S6 options-off | S6 decoyed | 37.4% | 39.0% | +33 / −0 |

   The decoyed exam gains little, as expected: 75% of its failures are
   look-alikes. A look-alike has the same types as the right tool, so it
   compiles, and the typechecker cannot separate the two.
5. **The model is often confidently wrong, which is why resampling
   underperforms.** Among the backoff rescues that reached the goal, the tool
   that fixed the program had a median probability of 0.22, and a quarter of
   them had under 0.05. Sampling at temperature 0.7 rarely draws a 5% option
   at the one slot that matters while keeping every other slot greedy. In 91
   of the 101 rescues the first alternative tried was the fix.

**New best on unseen worlds: 84.5% on the S6 plain exam.** That is run 3's
checkpoint (`models/tiny/runs/pod_s6split/runs/s6off_A0/best.pt` on
`models/tiny/data_cache_s6off`) with tool backoff. With greedy decoding it
scores 79.5%.

## Terms

- **Same-shape tools:** two tools with the same form but different
  entities, for example `(I:spot) -> OBJ:spot` and
  `(I:advertiser) -> OBJ:advertiser`, or two listers that take nothing and
  return `LIST OBJ:<entity>`. `fail_kinds.py` counts a swap between them as
  "tool: another signature", because the types differ.
- **A tool's entity:** the object its return type names, or else the entity
  of its first ID parameter.
- **Compile:** the host's typechecker accepts the program
  (`core.pipeline.build(...).compile_ok`). This is the same check
  `evaluate.py --best-of` uses.
- Models, exams and runs are as in R14 ("Terms", "The runs").

## Why these seven worlds

Share of each world's tasks whose first mistake is another entity's tool:

| world | tasks | run 3: A0, S6 off | run 1: A0, S5 | run 2: SPt, S5 | R13: A0, S6 | R13: SPt, S6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ci_cd | 49 | 46.9% | 63.3% | 8.2% | 42.9% | 14.3% |
| border_transit | 67 | 46.3% | 34.3% | 10.4% | 13.4% | 10.4% |
| comic_publisher | 61 | 39.3% | 41.0% | 6.6% | 8.2% | 3.3% |
| toy_store | 48 | 37.5% | 39.6% | 16.7% | 62.5% | 50.0% |
| radio_station | 52 | 26.9% | 26.9% | 9.6% | 23.1% | 1.9% |
| animal_shelter | 58 | 20.7% | 24.1% | 20.7% | 36.2% | 12.1% |
| wedding_planning | 45 | 20.0% | 24.4% | 42.2% | 28.9% | 4.4% |
| the other 35 | 1,620 | 0.6% | 4.2% | 9.7% | 27.3% | 10.6% |

Two explanations were checked and ruled out:

- **How alike the two tools' descriptions are.** In the seven worlds, the
  most similar pair of same-shape tools shares 0.64 of its non-entity words
  on average. In the other 35 worlds it shares 0.52. But winery's getters
  are worded identically ("fetch one vineyard rec by id" / "fetch one lot rec
  by id"), and run 3 fails winery on 1.8% of its tasks.
- **How the tokenizer splits entity names.** ad_platform has the same
  "advertiser" entity as radio_station and fails 2.5% of its tasks. Many
  clean worlds split their entity names into more pieces than the seven do.

Different models fail different worlds. R13's S6-trained A0 fails
toy_store, projects (48.5%) and donor_relations (49.0%). SPt trained on S5
fails wedding_planning (42.2%) and donor_relations (44.9%). But runs 1 and 3
fail the same seven worlds with different training rows. Both are A0 with
seed 0 and the same cache settings. So the pattern follows the model, not
the corpus. Whether it follows the seed or the architecture is not known.
A second A0 seed on the same rows would tell.

|---|---|

Field errors are now the largest group. The largest share of them, 39 of

and 29% with greedy decoding.

## Caveats

1. **The pattern was found on the plain exam, and the headline number is
   also measured there.** Backoff's two settings (three extra decodes, two
   runner-ups per slot) were chosen to match best-of-4's budget before any
   result was seen, not tuned. Finding 4 is the out-of-sample check.
2. **Backoff needs the typechecker at decode time.** That is the laptop, or
   any host that runs the planner. The pod bundle leaves the sandbox out, as
   R14 caveat 4 found for best-of-4. Generate greedily on the pod and apply
   backoff on the laptop.
3. **One seed per model**, as in R14. The +5.0 and +3.8 gains are 101 and 77
   tasks with none lost. Seed noise cannot produce that, because the only
   programs that change are ones that failed to compile.
4. **The script is an experiment, not part of `evaluate.py`.** It reuses
   `evaluate.Split`, `load_model` and `sample.ar_sample`, and copies
   `--best-of`'s compile check (`models/tiny/evaluate.py:162-173`). It has no
   tests.

## Next

1. **Put tool backoff into `evaluate.py`** as a `--backoff K` option beside
   `--best-of`, with a unit test on a hand-built non-compiling program. It is
   a small change, but it needs a plan first.
2. **SPt on the options-off rows** (R14 next-run 1, about $0.30). This result
   strengthens the case: SPt already avoids most of A0's seven-world failure,
   and backoff adds about 4 points on top of either model. A second A0 seed
   on the same rows can share the pod for little extra cost. It would give
   the new best model a seed spread and test whether the seven worlds follow
   the seed.
3. R14 next-run 2 (the options put back one at a time) and the handoff's
   generator-drift check are unchanged.
