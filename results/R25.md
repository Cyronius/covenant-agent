# R25: Ternlight can learn who-does-what; the plan's position table was the wrong way to give it word order

**Date:** 2026-09-30. **Branch:** `description-reading`. **Spend:** $0 (laptop
CPU). **Plan:** `.claude/plans/role-aware-reader.md`, steps 0-5. The owner: "do
everything other than the pod run". Step 6 (the pod run) is ready and not run.

**Terms.** *Reader*: the frozen sentence encoder that turns a text into one
384-number vector for the planner (today Ternlight-mini, 9.5M parameters,
ternary weights). *Role flip*: a change that keeps the words but changes
who-does-what ("move it to bob" -> "move it from bob", "copies A to B" ->
"copies B to A", "done" -> "not done"). *Flip cosine*: how alike the reader
says a sentence and its flip are; 1.000 means it can't tell them apart.
*Constant probe*: C0's $0 check (`chunk_probe.py`): does the reader, matching
the request's 4-word chunks against each constant, score the constants the
reference program uses above the ones it doesn't (pair accuracy, 50 = chance).

## The result

1. **The retrained reader tells roles apart, and reads general English at
   least as well as before.** The chosen reader, r5, against the shipped one,
   on held-out text it never trained on:

   | | shipped | r5 | gate |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | held-out role flips, flip cosine | 0.928 | **0.789** | <= 0.85 |
   | our own requests, flipped (never trained on) | 0.964 | **0.843** | (information) |
   | "assign card 6 to Bob" / "assign Bob to card 6" | 1.000 | **0.855** | lower than shipped |
   | "move it to bob" / "move it from bob" | 0.959 | **0.779** | lower than shipped |
   | shuffled sentences | 1.000 | **0.942** | < 0.95 |
   | rank agreement with the teacher, general text | 0.684 | **0.752** | >= 0.664 |
   | constant probe, cluttered | 87.1 | 86.2 | >= 86 |
   | constant probe, plain | 66.6 | 65.2 | >= 65.5 **(miss, 0.3)** |
   | constant probe, demo (10 tasks) | 84.8 | 83.8 | (information) |
   | "the oldest issue" / "the newest issue" | 0.596 | 0.653 | lower than shipped **(miss)** |

   Five of seven gates pass. The two misses are narrow: 0.3 points on the
   plain probe (about 9 of ~3,000 constant pairs), and one antonym pair the
   teacher itself scores 0.665. The plan's kill rule ("flips can't go below
   ~0.9 without losing more than a point on the probe") does **not** fire: r3
   below reaches 0.839 at a cost of 0.4 / 0.2 probe points. A 2-layer,
   256-wide ternary reader is big enough for roles.

2. **The plan's position table broke the reader; the fix was a different way
   in for word order.** The plan said: add a table of one learned vector per
   word position (r1). That reader learned roles (flip cosine 0.837) but
   dropped the plain constant probe from 66.6 to **50.9** (ID constants 92 ->
   69). With an absolute table, the same words read differently at a
   different offset: "record 7" alone sits at positions 1-2, inside the chunk
   "delete record 7 now" at 2-3. Zeroing r1's table brings the probe back to
   63.1 (`results/logs/role/probe_r1_nopos.json`). Readers r3-r5 instead add to
   each word a learned share of its neighbours' vectors (offsets -2..+2; 1,024
   numbers), so a phrase reads the same wherever it sits and word order still
   counts. Attention biases by word offset (r2) were also shift-safe but too
   weak: after 1,000 steps the "assign card 6 to Bob" pair was still 1.000,
   and the run was stopped.

3. **The teacher can't teach roles by itself.** Step 0: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 gives
   held-out flips 0.949 on average ("to bob" / "from bob" 0.980), the shipped
   reader 0.972 (`results/logs/role/teacher_probe.log`). Search-trained
   encoders mostly ignore roles, as bge-small did in C0. So training needs a
   push-apart term: a penalty when a sentence and its flip come out above 0.8.

4. **The shipped reader beats its own teacher at matching constants.** The
   teacher scores 84.8 / 62.8 / 87.8 on the probe's cluttered / plain / demo
   halves, against the shipped reader's 87.1 / 66.6 / 84.8
   (`results/logs/role/probe_teacher.json`). r1 with its table zeroed scores
   85.2 / 63.1, i.e. right at the teacher: copying the teacher more closely
   wears off the student's edge. From r2 on, an anchor term keeps each original
   sentence near the shipped reader's own vector, while the flips get the
   teacher copy and the push.

## The runs

All one epoch from the shipped weights, laptop CPU, 21-37 minutes each. Logs:
`results/logs/role/train_r*.log`, gates: `check_r*_v2.json` (all on the same
held-out set).

| run | word order | data | anchor, push | held flips | shuffled | probe clut / plain / demo | gates |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped | none | | | 0.928 | 1.000 | 87.1 / 66.6 / 84.8 | |
| r1 | position table (plan) | flips v1 | 0, 1 | 0.837 | 0.925 | 84.2 / **50.9** / 85.5 | 5/7 |
| r2 | attention bias by offset | flips v1 | 1, 1 | stopped: order not learned | | | |
| r3 | neighbour mix | flips v1 | 1, 2 | 0.839 | 0.972 | 86.7 / 66.4 / 81.8 | 5/7 |
| r4 | neighbour mix | flips v2 | 1, 2 | 0.803 | 0.896 | 86.2 / 64.2 / 78.4 | 5/7 |
| **r5** | neighbour mix | flips v2 | 1, 2, int4 in training | **0.789** | 0.942 | 86.2 / 65.2 / 83.8 | 5/7 |

- **Flips v1** (`tern_train.py flips`): 268,599 flips from 480,000
  `data/general` texts (WordNet, Google and AWS API docs, open tool
  descriptions; all permissive), a tenth of the source texts held out. Three
  families in equal shares: a role word swapped (to/from, before/after,
  with/without, more/less, first/last, into/out of), "not" added or dropped,
  two noun phrases swapped around a relation word or verb. 40% (and every
  text over 24 words) cut to a few words around the flip, as short as the
  planner's chunks.
- **Flips v2** adds WordNet antonyms in the same word form (oldest ->
  youngest, increases -> decreases; an inflected one must occur in the
  corpus) and, for 15% of texts, a shuffled copy copied from the teacher (word
  order alone). 403,869 rows. r3 failed the shuffle gate (0.972) and two hand
  pairs; these two are the direct data fixes.
- **int4 in training (r5)**: the shipped reader stores its word vectors in 4
  bits. r4 trained them at full precision and rounded at the end, which moved
  most hand pairs up (oldest/newest 0.628 -> 0.652, before/after March
  0.781 -> 0.832). r5 rounds them in every
  forward pass, gradients passed straight through.
- About 9% of the ternary weights changed sign or zero-ness in r4/r5. The
  plan's worry that rebuilt ternary weights would be too stiff to move was
  handled by a per-matrix learning rate proportional to each matrix's median
  weight.

## The rebuild (step 1)

`tern_reader.py` reads Ternlight's published `model-int4.bin` (Hugging Face
`wenshutang/ternlight`, MIT, sha256 `07d8cfdb...`; byte-identical to the
weights inside the npm package's wasm) into PyTorch, with its training weights
rebuilt as ternary value x the matrix's median weight. Against node's
`embed()`: minimum cosine 0.999993 on 1,521 texts (constants, fields, chunks,
descriptions, general text, inputs cut at 128 tokens). Training weights round
back to the exact published codes and scales, and the int4 word vectors to the
same rows (`test_tern_reader.py`). The constant probe run through the PyTorch
copy reproduces C0's node numbers exactly (87.1 / 66.6 / 84.8).

## Wired into C0 (step 5)

- `data_cache_c0/reader_role.pt`: r5's vectors for the cache's 180,698 texts,
  beside `reader.pt` (the shipped reader's). It records r5's sha256.
- `train.py --reader-file reader_role.pt` picks the table; the checkpoint
  remembers it (`Config.reader_file`), and `evaluate.py` loads it from there.
- `play.py --reader-live` takes a node dir or a reader file
  (`live_reader.open_reader`); `live_reader.check_reader` refuses a live reader
  other than the one that built the checkpoint's table. The demo server
  refuses a role-aware checkpoint (it runs only the shipped reader).
- `test_reader_lines.py`: r5 live on about 20 holdout rows gives the vectors its
  table holds (cosine > 0.999 on every constant, field, chunk, tool and
  request); the node reader is refused for that table. 14 tests pass.
- Smoke: both new arms train on CPU (128 rows), generate, and play the demo
  live.

## Not done

- **The engine.** Ternlight's WASM engine has neither a position table nor the
  neighbour mix. The mix is a small change: after the embedding lookup, add
  4 x 256 per-channel weights times the neighbouring words' vectors, before
  layer 1. The planner pipeline runs r5 in PyTorch until then.
- **Consistency of the role direction.** The push only makes a sentence and
  its flip differ; nothing yet checks that "to X" vs "from X" differ in the
  same direction across sentences. If the pod shows `c0_RLr` no better than
  `c0_RL`, that is the next thing to look at.
